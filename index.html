      Chocolate vs. everything else

      About half of each bag is chocolate, which tracks for this neighborhood. The difference is in the other half: she came home with more gummies and chewy candy, he came home with more lollipops and hard candy. I have theories about which houses he hit.

          How long will the candy last?

          A day-by-day guess at when each bag runs out. This is not a straight line. The first few days after Halloween are a free-for-all, school days slow things down (candy only comes out after dinner), and weekends and breaks speed things back up. Pick a weekend pace. There is also an option for Dad to take a piece or two after the kids go to bed, which I'm told is purely hypothetical.

          About this page

          We counted the candy on Nov. 1, 2026 and typed it into a spreadsheet: one row per kind of candy, one column per kid. The Girl is 4 and went as a Rainbow Fairy with Mom and Dad. The Boy is 9, went as Colonel Sanders, and trick-or-treated with friends (and, for a stretch, solo).

          The calorie numbers are rough estimates for a typical fun-size piece; I did not weigh anything. How the candy is grouped (chocolate, gummy, hard candy) is my own call, and I'm sure someone will disagree about Skittles. The page itself is one HTML file and a bit of D3, styled after FiveThirtyEight's 2016 election forecast because I liked it and it felt right for a two-candidate race.
